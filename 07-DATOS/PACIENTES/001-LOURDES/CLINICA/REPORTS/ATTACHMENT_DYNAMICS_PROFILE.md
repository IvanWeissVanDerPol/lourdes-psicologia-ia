## 2. The Cycle: "The Trap"

This dyad is caught in a classic **Pursuer-Distancer** cycle.

### Phase 1: The Trigger (Perceived Distance)

- Victor seeks autonomy or is busy (neutral event).
- **Patient Perception:** "He is pulling away. He doesn't love me. I am unsafe."
- _Audio Evidence:_ "Me siento más hipersensible que antes... porque te tengo muy cerca."

### Phase 2: The Protest (Hyperactivation)

- Patient attempts to bridge the gap through **Demand/Plead**.
- **Behavior:** Sending multiple voice notes (audio flooding), asking for reassurance, expressing distress.
- _Data Point:_ 5+ voice notes sent in rapid succession during conflict periods (e.g., File `00000696` series).

### Phase 3: The Withdrawal (Deactivation)

- Victor feels overwhelmed/criticized ("You are too much").
- **Behavior:** He withdraws further, stops replying, or gives short/cold answers.
- _Patient Reaction:_ "Perdón por pensar que me respondes fríamente." (File: `00001679` - _Self-Blame/Apology_)

### Phase 4: The Collapse & Repair

- Patient often takes the blame to restore connection.
- _Mechanism:_ **Self-Abandonment**. She apologizes for _his_ withdrawal to lower the tension.
- _Quote:_ "No sé si me expliqué bien... pero es lo que yo siento nomás." (Justifying her feelings).

---

## 3. Clinical Recommendation: "Secure Anchoring"

**Goal:** Discontinue the "Protest" behavior and establish self-soothing.

1.  **Stop the Chase:** Patient must learn to _pause_ when triggered. The urge to send "just one more voice note" is the anxiety speaking.
2.  **Externalize the Cycle:** Help them see "The Cycle" as the enemy, not each other.
3.  **Direct Communication:** Replace protest ("You never answer me!") with direct need ("I feel lonely and need a 5-minute call to feel connected").
